# Interview guide (45 minutes)

**Format:** one-on-one video call, semi-structured. Record only with consent. One person asks and, if possible,
a second person takes notes.

**Rules for the interviewer**
- Ask about **what happened**, not what they would do: "Tell me about the last time…", not "Would you…?"
- **Don't name the hypotheses.** Don't say "market fall", "panic", "payment failure" or "XIRR" until they do.
  If they never raise it, that is a finding.
- Follow the story, not the script. The sections are a checklist; the critical incident (section 4) gets the time.
- Useful follow-ups: *"What happened next?" · "What did you do then?" · "How did you feel at that point?" ·
  "Why was that important?" · "Can you remember a specific time?"* Stay silent for 3 seconds before moving on.
- Never give investment advice during or after the call, even if asked. Say: "I can't advise on that, but I'm
  happy to point you to the fund house's official page."

---

## 0 · Consent (2 min) — read aloud

> Thanks for making time. I'm researching how people experience their first year of SIP investing, for a
> product course project. There are no right or wrong answers — I want to understand your experience.
>
> - I won't ask for any account numbers, PAN, amounts or screenshots. If you mention amounts, ranges are fine.
> - Can I record this call? The recording is only for my notes, stays private, and is deleted after the project.
>   In my notes you'll be "P1", not your name.
> - You can skip any question or stop at any time.
> - I'm not selling anything and can't give investment advice.
>
> Is that okay?

Record the answer in the notes ("Consent to record: yes / no").

## 1 · Warm-up (3 min)

- What do you do, and how long have you been working?
- How do you usually handle money each month — what happens when your salary comes in?

## 2 · Why they started and what they expected (6 min) — *start motives, expectations*

- Take me back to when you started your first SIP. What made you start **then**?
- Who or what influenced you? (YouTube, friend, colleague, family, an app ad…)
- How did you choose the amount and the date?
- What did you expect would happen — after a few months, after a year?
  *Probe:* Did you have a number or outcome in mind? Where did it come from?
- Was there a goal behind it? *(H1: a goal and horizon to judge against)*

## 3 · The experience since: timeline (8 min) — *how behaviour changes*

- Let's draw your SIP journey from the day you started to today. What stands out month by month?
- When did it feel good? When did it feel bad or confusing?
- Has anything about your money or life changed since you started? (job, city, expenses)
  *(H4, H5: cash squeeze, account change)*

Note each event with a rough month. You'll use these to choose the moment for section 4.

## 4 · The critical incident (15 min) — *pause, reduce or stop*

Pick the moment from the timeline when continuing was most at risk. If they never considered stopping, ask:
"Has there been a time when you felt unsure about the SIP?" If still nothing, use the continuer probes at the end.

- Tell me about that moment in as much detail as you can. When was it? Where were you?
- What made you start thinking about it? **What did you see first?** *(trigger: returns, news, cash, a message)*
- What did you look at, in the app and outside it? *(H1, H6)*
- Who did you talk to or ask? What did they say? *(H2)*
  *Probe:* What answer would have settled it for you?
- What options did you think you had? *Probe, only if they don't mention them:* Did you know you could reduce
  the amount, skip a month or change the date? *(H4)*
- What did you decide, and how did you carry it out in the app? Was anything confusing?
- *If they stopped one SIP to start another:* How long was the gap? Did the new one actually start? *(H3)*
- Looking back, how do you feel about that decision? Was anything missing that would have helped you decide?
  *(H1)*

**Continuers' version:** Has the value ever gone down? What did you do when you saw it? What kept you going?
What do you think people who stop are missing?

## 5 · What they check and when (5 min) — *checking behaviour*

- How often do you open the app to look at the SIP? What prompts you?
- Walk me through what you look at, screen by screen (describe it; no need to show it).
- What does that number tell you? *(Let them explain returns or XIRR in their own words — H6.)*
- How do you feel right after checking?

## 6 · Outside the app (3 min) — *workarounds*

- When you're unsure about the SIP, where do you go? (YouTube, Reddit, friends, family, a finance person)
- What do you search for or ask? What do you trust, and why?

## 7 · Payment probe (3 min) — *Problem 1 (H5)*

- Has an instalment ever not gone through, or been delayed?
  - *If yes:* How did you find out? How long after? What did you have to do? Did the SIP continue afterwards?
- Is the SIP paid from the account your salary goes into? *(two-account setups)*
- *If they changed job or bank:* What happened to the SIP mandate?

## 8 · What would help them continue (3 min)

- If you could change one thing about the SIP experience in the first year, what would it be?
- What would make you more confident about continuing next year?

## 9 · Concept reaction (4 min) — only after everything above

Read this, or show the sketch in `concept_stop_sheet.md`:

> Imagine that when you tap "Stop SIP", before confirming, the app shows one screen with:
> your goal and the timeline you set when you started; how much you've invested so far and what your
> instalments have bought; in plain words, what stopping changes for that goal; and other options —
> reduce the amount, skip one month or change the date — alongside "Stop anyway".
> It doesn't recommend any fund and doesn't tell you what to do.

- What's your first reaction? What would you do on that screen?
- Would it have changed anything at the moment you described earlier? Why or why not?
- Is anything there that would annoy you or feel pushy?
- What's missing?

Don't defend or explain the concept. If they dislike it, ask why and move on.

## 10 · Close (1 min)

- Is there anything about your first year with SIPs I should have asked and didn't?
- Thank them. Confirm the voucher, if any. Ask if you can come back with a prototype in a week or two (Part 6).
